## The Journey Before Settling

Niri with Noctalia Shell is my current desktop environment (DE), not the first. I have my fair share of experience with various DE's and window managers (WM). In this section, I'll be sharing my experience and opinions in those DEs and WMs.

### Gnome
Gnome felt too much. I like my desktop clean and minimalist and Gnome lacked customization to remove some parts I didn't want. Perhaps it was due to Gnome being better for MacOS refugees. My girlfriend uses MacOS majority of the time and she felt more comfortable on Gnome than any other DEs.

### Hyprland
After Gnome, I jumped straight into customization freedom: Hyprland. That was a major mistake.

Back then I did not know the difference between DEs and WMs. I used Hyprland without a shell and thought I had to add all the service and daemons myself... which I did. I ended up with a spaghetti of intertwined config files and a lack of coherence between every part of my DE.

Despite all that, Hyprland was a fun experience. I skipped a lot of the learning but just using someone else's dotfiles (bad idea). The tiling was okay. I didn't really get to use it to it's full potential as I didn't need want a window smaller than half my screen *(foreshadowing)*.

### KDE Plasma
At this point I still did not know what DEs and WMs were so I assumed KDE Plasma was just a more complete desktop. 

KDE Plasma was what I used for the longest before switching to Niri. As a Windows 11 refugee, this felt the most familiar while also giving me a lot of freedom. I liked the fact that it just worked while providing me a UI for deep settings.

Although, I still preferred having some tiling.

### Niri without Noctalia Shell
To quench my tiling thirst, I installed Niri alongside KDE Plasma as a separate environment.

By this point I still have not learned my lesson about WMs. I ha to set up my own daemons an services again because I used Niri without a shell. This caused some daemons interfering with each other when I switch back to KDE Plasma so I ended up not using Niri as much.

Also, Niri's config file was very reminiscent of Hyprland's config. There were too many things in one file so it was hard to change something on the fly.
